# Documentary Editing Style

The default approach for documentary, mini-doc, brand-story, explainer and other story-led edits — whether building a CapCut project file, a Premiere/Resolve timeline, a Remotion composition, an edit plan, or reviewing a cut.

Core belief: what separates a filmmaker from a videographer is structure and feeling, not gear, pretty shots or transitions. Story first, sound second, visuals last.

Short-form (9:16 ads, reels) can stay tighter, but still gets a hook in the first seconds and at least one deliberate change of pace at the key beat.

---

## 1. Build order (always follow)

Duplicate the sequence/project at every stage (Cut 1, Cut 2, Skeleton, Build 1, Picture) so nothing is lost and progress is visible.

1. **Sort** footage into buckets (short project: Dialogue + B-roll; bigger: Interview / Vérité sync / B-roll / Archival / Music / SFX).
2. **Dialogue scrub — Cut 1.** Lay out every interview and vérité bite. Remove obvious fat; loose, not ruthless (e.g. 20 min → ~3 min).
3. **Dialogue clean-up — Cut 2.** Two passes before any B-roll:
   - Pass 1 (low-hanging fruit): false starts and restarted sentences, "you know"/"um"/"err", repeated small phrases ("and the, and the"), multiple possible endings → pick one.
   - Pass 2 (fine tune): even out the rhythm — trim long thinking pauses, smooth machine-gun bursts, keep pauses consistent so the speaker sounds confident and intentional.
   - Tighten toward target length (e.g. ~3 min → 1:45). Add text labels over each chunk naming its theme.
4. **Skeleton.** Reorder bites into a narrative spine. Think like a writer, not an editor.
   - Frame: **setup → conflict → resolution.** Conflict can be an aspiration, a struggle or an internal tension. Resolution can be emotional or musical rather than literal — "how do I want to leave the viewer?"
   - Run the **But/So test** (section 3).
   - Decide the **cold open** (section 3).
   - Treat it as play; take a break and return with fresh eyes.
5. **Audio-driven assembly — Build 1.** Add music, score, field recordings, SFX and silences. Edit it like a song with rise and fall. **Still no B-roll on the timeline.**
6. **Picture.** Only now lay in B-roll (section 6), archival, graphics and texture. Add on-location vérité sync at key moments to back up the interview. Seek extra footage for variety and novelty.
7. **Sound pass and polish** (section 5), then final review (section 8).

## 2. Pacing: know when NOT to cut fast

- Constant tight pacing (the short-form reflex) removes weight. If everything moves at one speed, nothing matters.
- Long form should feel like a roller coaster: accelerate then breathe, reveal then withhold, tension then release. Think **gears in a car** — motorway speed vs. town speed. Audiences crave variation.
- **Slow down at two places:**
  - **Complexity** — when a speaker packs dense ideas into one answer, don't simplify it and don't leave it intact. Split it into parts and insert space (B-roll, music, a beat) so one idea lands at a time.
  - **Emotion** — non-actors don't know when they've said something big; the editor must. Hold the shot longer before and after they speak, let reactions play, allow silence instead of filling it.
- Netflix sit-down move: after an emotional answer ends, linger on the B-cam angle almost uncomfortably long.
- The editor is the first audience: if it's hard to follow after 100 viewings, it's harder for a first-time viewer.
- Often the difference is just a few extra seconds in a few key places.

## 3. Story tools

**But/So test.** Retell the cut out loud with "but" or "so" between every beat. If two beats are joined only by "and then", the link is weak — reorder, cut, or add a beat that creates the but/so.

**Murch's Rule of Six** — the tiebreaker when unsure about a cut. Priority order: Emotion (~51%) > Story (~23%) > Rhythm (~10%) > Eye-trace (~7%) > 2D screen plane > 3D space of action. Honoring a higher item can hide flaws in lower ones, never the reverse. If a cut feels right emotionally, small continuity or eye-trace errors are acceptable.

**Jeopardy / open loops.** Audiences can't stand uncertainty; use that.
- Always ask: *what is at stake?* No stakes → no jeopardy → no drama.
- Don't answer questions too soon. Open loops (Will it work? Is he right? Why the pause?) and leave them open long enough to pull.
- Cut to the **listener's reaction**, not always the speaker; nudge reactions a few frames to land harder.
- Place the most doubtful or negative line at the **start or end** of a scene to set an undertone of unease.
- Slow down just before something critical; hold a beat after a revelation; cut away just before an answer when tension serves.
- Frame what could go wrong, not just what happens next. Plant doubt before the triumph ("is he still as good?").

**Cold open / hook.**
- Open with a conflict, mystery or striking visual within the first seconds, before titles.
- Keep it tight and make sure it ties back later in the story.
- Make clear who or what the film is about almost immediately (subject on screen + name card).

## 4. Music editing

Mood-matching a track and dropping it in is level one. Shape the music to the story.

- **Duet theory.** Dialogue and music take turns. Map the track's most emotional moments and place them in the gaps between dialogue "sync islands", right after a strong line — words deliver meaning, music reinforces it. Let the musical phrase finish; its length defines the pause. Never let the music's best moment peak under someone talking.
- **Musical element assignment.** Cut to individual instruments, not just the beat: a violin swell as someone looks over a landscape, a kick drum on a door slam or sudden move, a quirky clarinet on an awkward glance, a chorus drop as the subject stops talking and smiles.
- **Stems.** Split tracks into vocals / drums / bass / other (free tool: UVR). Mute instruments or vocals that fight dialogue; fade layers in and out instead of cutting between whole tracks.
- **Negative space.** Drop the music just before a key line so it lands with emphasis. If full silence is too jarring mid-video, keyframe a **low-pass filter** from high to low (or high-pass low to high) into the moment instead.
- **Sync points.** Sync a musical hit to the title card. Drop music entirely on simple location changes to keep rawness; bring it back when the story moves forward. Let the music turn exactly at the story's turn (e.g. the redemption line).
- **Levels:** music −5 to −25 dB; fade in.
- **Reverb tail ending:** cut where the track should end → step back ~10 frames, split → duplicate that tail into a compound/nested clip → inside, extend a muted copy a few seconds for room → apply reverb (smallish room, ~4000 ms reverb time, 50% wet) → drop the compound clip ~−5 dB to match level.

## 5. Sound design (Netflix-doc layering)

Sound is roughly half the experience; strip it and the same intro goes flat.

**Layering rule:** build every big sound from **low + mid + high** (e.g. swish = low whoosh + mid lighter/fire flicker + high whoosh).

**Key-match SFX** to the music: whooshes, hits and risers that clash with the music's key feel off even to untrained ears. Find keys (file name or Tunebat) and pitch-shift: 1 semitone = 1 piano key (e.g. sound in A, music in F → shift accordingly); fine-tune in cents (100 cents = 1 semitone). When no key info, sweep pitch and trust your ears.

| Element | Use | Level | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Camera clicks / shutters | Image sequences, punchy hard cuts | −25 to −40 dB (often −30/−35) | Alternate 2+ click sounds; bigger camera-shot sound on the last image. Convert mono SFX to stereo |
| Risers | Build into image bursts / big visual moments | ~−25 dB | End on the impact |
| Whooshes / swishes | Any on-screen move (scale, rotation, position) | ~−25 dB | One go-to whoosh: ~60% speed + ~−5 semitones = low layer; copy at ~120% + ~+4–5 semitones = high layer. Peak on the movement. Lighter/light-leak sound on film-burn transitions (~−22 dB) |
| Bass hits | Release of tension, title cards | layered −25 / −30 / −35 dB | Start a few frames before the cut; tiny fade-in, earlier fade-out |
| Impact hits | Hype sequences | to taste | Add an extra impact on every music hit / punch / transition |
| Reverse wash / subsonic drop | Into a key line, title or fade to black | to taste | Classic doc button |
| Archival / news voice bed | Context under a sequence | ~−15 to −20 dB | Isolate voice with a vocal remover; split into segments; light EQ to muffle so it sits inside the mix; can bridge off a title card |

Process truth: it's trial and error — audition, swap sounds that don't fit, adjust a few dB at a time.

Free sources: YouTube Audio Library, Freesound, Musicbed, a vocal remover, UVR for stems.

## 6. Picture, B-roll and transitions

- **J-cuts and L-cuts everywhere.** J-cut: hear the next speaker/scene before seeing it. L-cut: let audio continue over the next shot. Use them to introduce speakers, bridge interview to B-roll and smooth jump cuts. Check: you should feel the overlap but not see it.
- **Literal-match B-roll.** Show what's being said ("solid ground" → on the ground; "stand-up" → standing; "manhandled" → him tipping a partner over).
- **Mood-match B-roll.** Pick shots that mirror the emotional state: deflated posture for the low point, sweat and exhaustion for struggle, walking from dark into light for the turn.
- **Cut on action** — switch camera angles on punches, steps and gestures; cut location shots on audio beats.
- **Establish scale** for big events: crowd, venue, fireworks, location shots.
- **Hype / finale sequence:** black-and-white for grit; speed clips up slightly so actions land on music hits; impact SFX on each hit; tight close-ups; subtle camera shake on the final shot; reverse wash → fade to black → end card.
- Use slow motion (shot at high frame rate) for emphasis beats.
- Respect rights: don't use footage you can't license (e.g. league/broadcast footage); substitute with training or re-enactment B-roll.

## 7. Johnny Harris / textured explainer look (CapCut recipe)

For archival, history, explainer and "Vox-style" pieces. Settings below are CapCut names; translate for other NLEs or Remotion.

- **Typewriter titles:** typewriter-style font, in-animation "Retro typer" (~3 s). Split just before the last frame; keyframe a slight scale-down (zoom-out) on the first part and a slightly larger scale on the held tail; compound both.
- **Texture base:** film-dust overlay as background; adjustment layer with contrast around −18 (whites around −34 on photo sections).
- **Archival photos:** desaturate fully to black-and-white; layer memo/document cutouts between them; "90s" style effect; compound and speed up (e.g. 10x) for a rapid image burst (pair with camera clicks, section 5).
- **Documents/memos:** invert with a flipped curve if needed; white stroke via scaled-down layer over a white background + mask; compound, duplicate per page and use Replace Clip inside the compound to swap pages without redoing edits; eased keyframed zoom-out.
- **Transitions:** film-roll overlay trimmed across the cut, blend mode Screen; add "TV warble" / "Bad TV" style effects matched to the text cuts.
- **Profile cards:** white background, rounded-corner rectangle mask on the portrait, bold name font + regular role font, vertical text along the image edge.
- **3D maps:** style the map (e.g. Snazzy Maps + Photopea), apply a 3D tilt effect, trim any built-in fade; route/line assets in Multiply blend, revealed by keyframing a split mask; cross marks built the same way, duplicated and flipped; compound everything, then keyframe a push-in onto the target; set keyframe curves to ease.
- **Workflow:** compound clips aggressively for reuse and clean keyframing; copy/paste adjustment layers for a consistent look.

## 8. Review checklist (use when reviewing a cut)

Structure
- [ ] Clear subject and hook in the first seconds?
- [ ] Setup → conflict → resolution present (even if emotional/musical)?
- [ ] Every beat joined by but/so, not "and then"?
- [ ] Stakes stated or felt? Open loops held long enough, then paid off?

Pacing
- [ ] Pace varies — fast stretches and breathing stretches?
- [ ] Dense explanations split with space between ideas?
- [ ] Emotional beats held before and after; reactions allowed to play; silence kept?

Dialogue
- [ ] False starts, fillers, repeats and stray endings removed; rhythm even?
- [ ] J/L-cuts used for speaker intros and B-roll bridges?

Music and sound
- [ ] Music peaks land in dialogue gaps, not under speech?
- [ ] Specific instrument moments tied to specific picture moments?
- [ ] Negative space or filter sweep before the key line?
- [ ] Transitions layered low/mid/high and key-matched; levels in range?
- [ ] Clean ending (reverb tail or reverse wash → black)?

Picture
- [ ] B-roll matches the words or the mood — never generic filler?
- [ ] Cuts on action/beats; finale synced to music hits?
- [ ] Texture/graphics consistent (adjustment layer, overlays) and rights-safe?

When giving notes, cite timecodes and name the principle (e.g. "0:42 — emotional beat cut off; hold 1–2 s after the line and let the reaction play").
